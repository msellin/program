How Terav plans training

Method: your active program picks the scheme. Strength programs use 5/3/1 with training-max-based progression. Aerobic programs use zone-based prescriptions. Skill programs use tier gates. All of them progress off your logged data, not a calendar.

Shape: every program is chunked into phases. Phases advance when the progression rule for that phase says green — a combination of adherence, symptom state, and any retest metric the program defines.

Adjustments: the engine proposes changes after each session and each morning check. Nothing is applied until you tap Accept — you can ignore a proposal and the plan holds.

Endurance terms

Z1 / Zone 1. Very easy. Nose-breathing pace, could hold a conversation the whole session. Roughly 65-75% max HR. This is where mitochondrial and capillary adaptation happens; feels slow because it is.

Z2 / Zone 2. Easy-moderate. Just above conversational — you could talk in short sentences but not sing. Roughly 75-85% max HR / below LT1. The bulk of endurance base work.

Threshold (LT2 / MLSS). The pace where blood lactate stabilises at a moderately high level — sustainable for ~60 min in trained athletes. HR usually 85-92% max. Not race pace, not easy.

VO2max intervals (Norwegian 4x4). 4 minutes at ~90-95% max HR, 3 minutes easy, repeated 4 times. The most efficient protocol for raising VO2max in trained-but-not-elite athletes (Helgerud 2007).

500m split (rowing / erg). Time to complete 500 metres, expressed as mm:ss. A 2K in 7:35 = ~1:53.7 avg split. Lower is faster. The universal rowing pace metric.

Taper. Deliberate reduction in training volume (usually 40-60%) with intensity held for the final 1-2 weeks before a test. Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis: correctly done, a taper adds ~3% to peak performance. Under-taper OR over-taper both cost.

Strength terms

TM (Training Max). The number all percentages calculate from. Roughly 90% of a solid 5RM or 85% of your 1RM. Deliberately submaximal — that headroom is what lets you progress without stalling. If TM is 110 kg, top set week 1 is 85% × 110 = 93.5 kg for 5+ reps.

1RM. Your one-rep max. Only tested at the end of a full 4-cycle block and at program-defined peak points.

5RM. Heaviest weight you can move for 5 clean reps. Used in evaluation week to set the initial TM. Formula: TM = 5RM × 0.90.

Sets × reps. “5×5” = five sets of five. Rest 2-3 min between working sets on main lifts.

AMRAP. As Many Reps As Possible. Week 1 top set is 5+; do at least 5, then go until form breaks. This is where strength jumps come from.

FSL (First Set Last). After the top set, do 5 more sets of 5 at the first working weight of the day. Volume without more nervous-system output.

Cycle. One 4-week 5/3/1 block: week 1 (5s), week 2 (3s), week 3 (1+ AMRAP), week 4 (deload).

Deload. Week 4 of every cycle. Low load (40/50/60% TM). Not optional. It's what lets you keep progressing.

RPE (0–10) — Rate of Perceived Exertion. How hard the set felt = how many reps you had left in the tank.

10 = failure · 9 = one rep left · 8 = two left · 7 = three · 6 = four · 5 = half-effort

Example: 90 × 5 @ RPE 5 means you did 5 reps with 90 kg and could have done 5 more. The app reads RPE to decide next session's bump — RPE ≤ 5 gets +10 kg, RPE 7 gets +5, RPE 9 holds, RPE 10 usually means TM reset.

Target RPE by scheme: warm-ups 4-6 · 5/3/1 top set week 1 = 7-8 · week 2 = 8 · week 3 AMRAP = 9-10 · FSL 5×5 = 6-7 · deload = 4-5.

Reset rule. If you fail to hit reps on the week-3 top set, drop TM by 10% next cycle. Normal — don't muscle through failed reps.

How to use the tabs

Today. Today's prescribed session. Strength shows exercises with weight, reps, RPE per set, plate breakdown and a bar visual. Aerobic / rowing shows the block prescription plus a session-log card (duration, HR, splits, watts).

Week. The 7-day rhythm. Today is highlighted. Tap any date to view its session.

Progress. Training maxes, milestones, retest metrics + trend charts. Insights tab surfaces weekly narrative and program-specific progress.

History. Activity heatmap, symptom trend, strength progression, and an expandable day log with set-level detail.

Profile. Signed-in identity, active plan(s), and a compact menu.

These are the bottom-nav tabs. The rest live behind the ⋮ menu in the top right:

Programs. The catalog. Browse, preview, start a program.

Check. Morning symptom scoring. Save it and today's prescription auto-adjusts (amber −5%, red −10%).

Extras. Accessories, home rehab, cardio / conditioning blocks — no calendar pressure.

Report. Specialist-facing summary. Print / save as PDF for a clinician or coach.

Data. Manage data — import, export, wipe local.

Evidence. The full bibliography behind every program, proposal, and progression rule in the app. If a claim can't be traced back to something here, it's an engineering choice, not cited.

Guide. This page.

Green / Amber / Red

Symptom state, judged by the morning check the day after training.

Green. Nothing above 3/10, baseline within 24h. Progress load. TM +5-7.5 kg at cycle end.

Amber. A 4-5/10 or morning stiffness over 30 min. Hold load. Repeat the week.

Red. Painful click, night pain, gait change, or anything over 5. Back off two steps. TM -10%. Three ambers in a row = same action. Real red flags = clinician, not the app.

Red flags — stop the app, call a clinician

  • Pain that wakes you at night, or is worse at rest than with movement
  • Any pain that shortens your stride or changes how you walk
  • Sharp catch, click or lock in a joint under load
  • New neurological symptoms — numbness, tingling, weakness that doesn't recover
  • Morning stiffness over 30 min that responds to movement not rest (screen for inflammatory pattern)
  • Any injury with a clear mechanism you don't bounce back from in 72 h

Programs that already carry a specific clinical context (e.g. the anterior-hip case study) apply extra program-specific red flags on top of this general list.
