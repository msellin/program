# Motor Learning + Skill Acquisition — Primary Source Evidence Base

*Source report for skill-program architecture and the multi-dimensional generation strategy. 40+ primary studies with effect sizes.*

## The pillars

### 1. Motor learning stages
- Fitts PM, Posner MI 1967 — cognitive → associative → autonomous stages (descriptive)
- Bernstein NA 1967, *The Co-ordination and Regulation of Movements* — mastery of redundant DoF; freeze → release → exploit
- Newell KM 1985 — constraints-led framework: organismic, task, environmental constraints
- Karni A et al. 1998, PNAS 95:861-868 — fast (within-session) vs slow (across-session) learning; M1 remapping over ~3 weeks
- Doyon J, Benali H 2005, Curr Opin Neurobiol 15:161-167 — cortico-striatal vs cortico-cerebellar loop
- Krakauer JW et al. 2019, Compr Physiol 9:613-663 — best modern synthesis: use-dependent + error-based + reinforcement + strategic learning simultaneously
- Wolpert DM, Ghahramani Z 2000, Nat Neurosci 3(Suppl):1212-1217 — forward + inverse internal models

### 2. Contextual interference
- Shea JB, Morgan RL 1979, JEP Human Learning 5:179-187 — foundational: blocked better in acquisition, random better in retention
- Brady F 2004, PMS 99:116-126 — meta-analysis d=0.38 overall; d=0.57 lab, d=0.19 applied/sport
- Merbah S, Meulemans T 2011, Psychologica Belgica 51:15-48 — robust in lab, moderated by skill level + task similarity in field
- Wulf G, Shea CH 2002, Psychonom Bull Rev 9:185-211 — **crucial: principles from simple lab skills don't always generalize to complex skills; reduce challenge early, add later**
- Zeng X et al. 2024, Sci Rep 14 — modern meta confirming moderate CI benefit for retention

### 3. Specificity of practice
- Henry FM 1968 — specificity hypothesis; motor abilities are task-specific
- Proteau L, Marteniuk RG, Lévesque L 1992, QJEP 44A:557-575 — learning specific to sensory conditions of practice
- Kerwin DG, Trewartha G 2001, MSSE 33:1182-1188 — handstand hold characterized as wrist-torque-dominated

### 4. Variability + variability of practice
- Schmidt RA 1975, Psychol Rev 82:225-260 — schema theory (variability builds recall + recognition schemas)
- Wulf G, Shea CH 2005, JMB 37:85-102 — schema theory critical reappraisal
- Wulf G, Shea CH 2002, Psychonom Bull Rev 9:185-211 — complex-skill implications: constant → variable progression

### 5. Attentional focus
- Wulf G, Höß M, Prinz W 1998, JMB 30:169-179 — external focus outperforms internal (stabilometer, ski-simulator)
- Wulf G 2013, IntRev Sport Ex Psychol 6:77-104 — 15-year review: external focus reliably better across ~100 studies
- McNevin NH et al. 2003, Psychol Res 67:22-29 — farther external targets → larger effect
- Wulf G, Lewthwaite R 2016, PBR 23:1382-1414 — OPTIMAL theory (external focus + enhanced expectancies + autonomy)
- Halperin I et al. 2019, Front Sports Act Living 1:7 — weightlifting: external focus improves 1RM, force, velocity in most studies

### 6. Feedback frequency + type
- Salmoni AW, Schmidt RA, Walter CB 1984, Psych Bull 95:355-386 — guidance hypothesis: 100% KR hurts retention
- Winstein CJ, Schmidt RA 1990, JEP LMC 16:677-691 — 50% faded KR ≥ 100% during acquisition; better on retention
- Kernodle MW, Carlton LG 1992, JMB 24:187-195 — for complex tasks: KP > KR
- Chiviacowsky S, Wulf G 2002, RQES 73:408-415 — self-controlled KR beats yoked schedules
- Chiviacowsky S, Wulf G 2005, RQES 76:42-48 — post-trial choices > pre-trial

### 7. Deliberate practice + expertise
- Ericsson KA, Krampe RTh, Tesch-Römer C 1993, Psych Rev 100:363-406 — original deliberate practice paper (elite violinists ~10,000 h)
- Macnamara BN, Hambrick DZ, Oswald FL 2014, Psych Sci 25:1608-1618 — meta-analysis: **deliberate practice explains 26% of variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, <1% professions.** 10,000-hour rule not defensible.
- Macnamara BN, Maitra M 2019, Royal Soc Open Sci 6:190327 — direct replication of 1993 failed to reproduce strong effect

### 8. Consolidation + spacing
- Karni A et al. 1998, PNAS (as above)
- Walker MP et al. 2002, Neuron 35:205-211 — sleep-dependent motor consolidation, ~20% overnight gain
- Walker MP et al. 2003, Nature 425:616-620 — dissociable stages of memory consolidation
- Robertson EM, Pascual-Leone A, Miall RC 2004, Nat Rev Neurosci 5:576-582 — 4-6h post-practice consolidation window vulnerable to similar-task interference
- Shea CH et al. 2000, Human Mov Sci 19:737-760 — spacing across days > massing within day

### 9. Skill assessment (gymnastics)
- Sleeper MD, Kenyon LK, Casey E 2012, Int J Sports Phys Ther 7:124-138 — Gymnastics Functional Measurement Tool, test-retest r=0.99, construct validity on 105 female gymnasts 6-18
- Sleeper MD 2016 — extended reliability + age-referenced norms
- Handstand-specific biomechanics: Baker et al. 2024 systematic review; Farana et al. 2025

### 10. Prerequisite gating
- Sands WA 2000, Sports Med 30:359-373 — women's gymnastics injury prevention: skill-readiness assessment
- Gabbett TJ 2016, BJSM 50:273-280 — acute-to-chronic workload ratio predicts injury (general mechanism)
- **No known RCT compares prerequisite-gated vs free progression for handstand walk, muscle-up, or HSPU** — coach folklore, not empirically tested. Flag honestly.

### 11. Multi-dimensional progression models
- Newell KM 1985 constraints-led framework
- Kelso JAS 1995, *Dynamic Patterns* — sub-skills stabilize on their own attractors
- **No head-to-head "component" vs "linear" comparison for handstand walk / muscle-up / HSPU programming.** Real research gap.

### 12. Individual variation in skill acquisition
- Ackerman PL 1988, JEP Gen 117:288-318 — cognitive ability predicts early stage; perceptual speed middle; psychomotor autonomous
- Wu HG et al. 2014, Nat Neurosci 17:312-321 — 3× variance in visuomotor rotation learning; correlates with baseline motor variability
- Voelcker-Rehage C, Willimczik K 2006, Psychol Res 70:250-264 — older adults slower to learn but similar retention plateau

## What the literature DOES NOT decide — flag as engineering choices

Per the cross-cutting table from the research report:

| Design question | Literature verdict |
|---|---|
| Daily vs every-other-day handstand practice | Inference from consolidation + spacing lit; no direct trial |
| Session length (30 vs 60 min) | No strong evidence at this granularity |
| Exact reps per drill | No robust dose-response research for gymnastic skills |
| Prerequisite thresholds (30s vs 60s hold before walk) | Coaching folklore, not tested |
| Retest cadence (weekly, bi-weekly) | No RCT |
| Number of concurrent skills in program | Constraints-led theory supports parallelism; quantitative data absent |
| Specific cue phrasing | External-focus principle is directional; wording is craft |

Every one of these ships in the app's rationale metadata with `source: "engineering"` — never marketed as science.

## Architecture principles (backed by literature)

Ten principles for the multi-dimensional generation strategy in `dev/active/saas-launch/architecture-v2.md`:

1. **Sub-skill independence**: track hold, walk, turns, obstacles as separate state variables (Newell 1985; Henry 1968; Proteau 1992)
2. **CI gated by phase**: blocked weeks 1-2, random week 3+ (Wulf & Shea 2002; Shea & Morgan 1979)
3. **External-focus cues**: standardized in drill library (Wulf 1998, 2013)
4. **Reduced-frequency, self-controlled feedback**: post-set optional; KP > KR for form-critical (Winstein & Schmidt 1990; Chiviacowsky & Wulf 2002)
5. **Daily short > infrequent long** for skill/balance (Karni 1998; Walker 2003; Shea 2000)
6. **Constant → variable progression** (Wulf & Shea 2002; Schmidt 1975)
7. **Prerequisite gating** as injury-mitigation heuristic (Sands 2000; Gabbett 2016) — plausible not proven
8. **No cohort ETAs**: individual variance is large (Ackerman 1988; Wu 2014)
9. **Assessment battery declared + versioned** (Sleeper 2012 as reference)
10. **10,000-hour heuristic not defensible** (Macnamara 2014; Macnamara & Maitra 2019)

## Non-peer-reviewed sources explicitly flagged as anecdotal (NOT evidence)

- Uzunov V 2008 "Four Stage Training Model" — coaching journal, non-peer-reviewed
- FIG Age Group Development Program — governing-body syllabus, consensus-based
- USA Gymnastics J.O. Compulsory levels — syllabus, not tested
- Balyi's Long-Term Athlete Development — under-evidenced "windows of trainability" for children
- GymnasticBodies (Sommer), Bar Brothers, Progressive Calisthenics — commercial, not peer-reviewed
