# Handstand Skill Acquisition & Loading — Primary Source Evidence Base

*Source report for Handstand Walk program design and evidence_base. 15+ primary studies, honest gap-flagging.*

## Executive summary

The primary-source literature on the handstand is smaller and more recent than most trained athletes assume. **MacDonald, Baker, Gu & Ugbolue 2025** (Frontiers systematic review, 21 studies 1988-2025) is the current best synthesis. Three defensible claims:
- Balance in a handstand is dominated by a wrist strategy (analogous to ankle strategy in upright stance)
- Elite hand-balancers differ from novices mainly in medial-lateral COP control, not just strength
- The empirical base for prerequisite thresholds is thin — most coach folklore is unverified

**2025 adult-practitioner survey (n=321):** chronic wrist pain in 56.7%, but **no significant association with weekly training hours, frequency, warm-up, or brace use**. Risk is technique-driven, not volume-driven.

## Strongest evidence — cite freely

- **Wrist strategy dominates balance** (Kerwin & Trewartha 2001, Blenkinsop 2017)
- **Elite vs novice: medial-lateral COP + stiffening strategy** (Sobera 2019; Baker 2025 systematic review)
- **Progressive 4-position EMG ladder** validated (Kinoshita 2022)
- **Video KP after ~5 reps** improves motor skill + self-assessment (Potdevin 2018 — n=18 children, 8-week gymnastics)
- **Self-controlled feedback** ("review clip" button) outperforms forced (Chiviacowsky & Wulf 2002, 2005; Grand 2015)
- **Combined tactile-verbal + visual feedback** best (Rohleder & Vogt 2018)
- **Wrist WB tolerance drops from age 45** (Barlow 2020, n=465 healthy adults)
- **Straight-arm press to handstand = 3× BW shoulder moment** (Sadowski 2021, PLOS One) — don't program early
- **Shoulder pain during handstand walk = technique-linked, not load-linked** (2026 Scientific Reports, 237 stance phases): pain-associated practitioners show shorter effective arm length + greater axial torsional work

## Engineering choices — must flag as choice, not evidence

- 60-s wall hold before freestanding — coach folklore, untested
- Weekly wrist volume ≤20-30 min for beginner (Wiesinger 2019 tendon-adaptation window is the mechanism basis, but no direct dose-response)
- Daily short sessions — theory-based (Karni 1998, Walker 2003)
- 180° shoulder flexion prerequisite — analogous to weightlifting 170° cutoff
- Hollow-body 30-60s prerequisite — coach consensus
- 15-s freestand as pirouette gate — no evidence
- Overhead press transfer to handstand — no RCT

## Non-negotiables for a shipped program

1. **Shoulder pain during a session ends the session** (Sci Reports 2026 pain-associated technique data — do not train through it)
2. **Osteoporosis = freestanding contraindicated** (bail failure loads distal radius / hip / vertebra — common fracture sites)
3. **Uncontrolled hypertension = defer inversions** (transient systolic increases documented in yoga-inversion research)
4. **Wrist volume cap 20-30 min/week weeks 1-2**, ≤10%/week increment thereafter — respects tendon-adaptation timeframe (Wiesinger 2019)

## Tiered intake — defensible tier structure

- **Tier A — Beginner adult, no prior handstand.** Screening: painless wrist extension ≥70°, shoulder flexion ≥170°, no osteoporosis, no acute injury. Start at Kinoshita position 1 (90° incline).
- **Tier B — Wall handstand ≥30s, no freestanding.** Enter at Kinoshita position 3 (elbow stand) + wall balance-search.
- **Tier C — Freestanding ≥15s.** Enter handstand-walking initiation drills (Ferrari 2021 CoP-shift work).
- **Tier D — Walks 10m+.** Advanced: turns, obstacles, precision path. Multi-block arc.

## Realistic outcomes per tier (engineering choice, coach-consensus corroborated)

- **Tier A (8 weeks):** stable Kinoshita position 3-4 hold; unassisted brief freestand kick attempts
- **Tier B (8 weeks):** first 5-15s freestand hold; first walk attempts on wall
- **Tier C (8 weeks):** 3-5 continuous walk steps, extended by short shuttles
- **Tier D (8 weeks):** first pirouette OR obstacle traverse OR precision-path work

## KPIs the app should defend

- Freestanding hold time (weekly max + median)
- Wall-to-freestand transfer ratio (freestand seconds ÷ wall seconds per week)
- Wrist symptom score trend (Vidal-Torija 2025 variable)
- Video-review self-selection frequency (motivation proxy, Chiviacowsky & Wulf)
- For Tier C+: continuous walk-step count (Ferrari-derived)

## Weekly structure — evidence-scaffolded

- 4-6 sessions/week, 10-15 min active handstand time each
- Wrist-conditioning warm-up: 3-5 min graded loading (Wiesinger 2019)
- Session A: position-blocked (Kinoshita ladder)
- Session B: interleaved positions + entries (Shea CI)
- Video KP available on demand, not forced
- Post-session: hold-time, freestand attempts, wrist symptom score
