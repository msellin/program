- **Rønnestad & Hansen 2020** — short intervals vs long intervals in cyclists. Anchors the 4×4 / short-interval alternation.
- **Buchheit & Laursen 2013 (Parts I + II)** — time-at-VO2max framework and practical HIIT programming.
- **Helgerud 2007** — the canonical Norwegian 4×4 anchor (reused from Block 1).

## The pillars

### 1. Threshold (LT2 / MLSS) as the Block 2 headline
- **Joyner MJ, Coyle EF 2008, J Physiol 586(1):35-44** — VO2max plateaus for well-trained athletes; threshold and economy continue improving. Anchors why threshold shift is the retest metric, not VO2max.
- **Billat V 2000, Sports Med 31(1):13-31** — Interval training review, aerobic focus.
- **Billat V, Sirvent P, Py G, et al. 2001, Sports Med 33(6):407-426** — Maximal lactate steady state concept.
- **Faude O, Kindermann W, Meyer T 2009, Sports Med 39(6):469-490** — Lactate threshold concepts review. Anchors the threshold framing.
- **Pilegaard H et al. 2000, AJP 278(4):E571-E579** — MCT1 upregulation. Direct anchor for threshold cruise as Block 2's primary driver.

### 2. Interval format — Rønnestad's short vs long
- **Rønnestad BR, Hansen J, et al. 2020** — Short intervals (5×3, 8×2) accumulate more time-at-VO2max per session for well-trained cyclists. Long intervals (4×5, 4×8) produce comparable adaptation with less monotony. Anchors the block's 4×4 / short-interval alternation across weeks 4, 7.
- **Buchheit M, Laursen PB 2013 (Part I) — Sports Med 43(5):313-338** — Time-at-VO2max framework. Cardiopulmonary emphasis.
- **Buchheit M, Laursen PB 2013 (Part II) — Sports Med 43(10):927-954** — Practical HIIT programming, anaerobic + neuromuscular considerations. Supports Z3 optional session for Push tier.
- **Bishop D, Girard O, Mendez-Villanueva A 2008, Sports Med 41(9):741-756** — Repeated-sprint recommendations. Supports short-interval format.

### 3. Norwegian 4×4 anchor — reused from Block 1
- **Helgerud J et al. 2007, MSSE 39(4):665-671** — 4×4 min at 90-95% HRmax produced VO2max +7.2%, SV +10% over 8 weeks. Canonical anchor for the block_norwegian_4x4 session.
- **Wisløff U et al. 2007, Circulation 115(24):3086-3094** — 12 weeks 4×4 in HF patients: VO2peak +46%, LV EF +35%. Reinforces the cardiac remodelling stimulus.
- **Astorino TA et al. 2013, JSCR 27(1):138-145** — HIIT dose-response for VO2max. Supports the expected +5-10% additional VO2max in Block 2.

### 4. Volume-vs-intensity — anchor for Z1 floor rising
- **Bishop DJ, Botella J, Granata C 2019, Physiology 34(1):56-70** — Volume drives mitochondrial content beyond a threshold; intensity drives peak capacity. Anchors the Z1 volume floor rising across Block 2.
- **Konopka AR et al. 2014, J Gerontol A 69(4):371-378** — Enzyme activity keeps climbing at week 12. Anchors additional gains at 8-week Block 2 boundary.

### 5. Polarised distribution — Seiler + Stöggl
- **Seiler S 2010, IJSPP 5(3):276-291** — 80/20 easy/hard. Anchors why Block 2 keeps two hard sessions (not three) and preserves Z1 volume.
- **Stöggl T, Sperlich B 2014, Front Physiol 5:33** — Polarised outperforms threshold-heavy. Reinforces the Z1 volume floor.

### 6. HR zone methodology — from Block 1
- `nes_2013_hunt` — HUNT formula 211 − 0.64 × age
- `tanaka_2001` — HRmax formula meta
- `rogers_2021_dfa` — DFA a1 = 0.75 as VT1 anchor
- Karvonen 1957 — %HRR foundational (referenced in prose, not in citations.json as its own entry)

### 7. Taper — Mujika & Padilla
- **Mujika I, Padilla S 2000, Sports Med 30(2 & 3)** — Taper literature: intensity preserved, volume down 40-60%, session count -1. Anchors the week 9-10 taper design.
- **Coyle EF et al. 1984, JAP 57(6):1857-1864** — Detraining timecourse. Informs the Foundation-tier 2-week re-entry ramp.

### 8. Individual variation
- `bouchard_1999_heritage` — 47% heritability of VO2max response, ~10× range.
- `ross_2015` — Non-response at 50% intensity drops to 0% at 75%. Informs Push tier's third-session option for high-cardio users.

### 9. Concurrent-strength policy (tightened vs Block 1)
- `hickson_1980` — original interference data.
- `wilson_loenneke_2012` — concurrent meta.
- **Fyfe JJ et al. 2016, Front Physiol 7:487** — Endurance VOLUME (not intensity) drives interference. Directly anchors why concurrent-strength cap tightens in Block 2's peak-volume weeks 5-7.
- `schumann_2022` — SMD -0.28 explosive strength. Anchors 'no PR chasing'.
- `robineau_2016` — 6h separation preserves both adaptations.
- `eddens_2018` — +6.91% lower-body strength when lifting precedes endurance.
- `morton_2018` — Protein floor 1.62 g/kg/day.
- `impey_2018` — Fuel resistance sessions ("Fuel for the Work Required").

## Engineering choices — must flag

Per program's `engineering_choices_flagged`:

- Threshold expansion 3×8 → 3×15 min across 8 weeks — mirrors coach-consensus threshold blocks, not RCT-derived progression.
- Alternating 4×4 and short-interval formats week-by-week — Rønnestad 2020 supports both but doesn't specify weekly alternation as optimum.
- Z3 optional third session for Push tier — pragmatic; Buchheit & Laursen 2013 support the zone but not exact placement.
- 20-min TT as threshold anchor — widely used FTP protocol convention, not a specific RCT.
- Foundation-tier 2-week re-entry ramp — engineering; Coyle 1984 detraining informs the concept.
- Week 4 mid-block retest — coach-consensus, not RCT.
- Z1 volume floor +30% by week 5-6 — Bishop 2019 supports the direction, exact percentage is engineering choice.
- 48h between hard sessions — supported by recovery literature but not specifically threshold vs VO2max.
- 10% weekly mileage cap for runners — Nielsen 2014 injury dose-response supports the direction; exact 10% is coaching folklore.

## Non-negotiables for a shipped program

1. Users without Block 1 (or equivalent base) are gated at intake — routes to Block 1.
2. Unmanaged hypertension → clinician clearance first.
3. Post-COVID with persistent HR elevation → do not begin.
4. Any joint / tendon flare → stay on low-impact modality or defer.
5. Two red days in a week → skip that week's VO2max session.

## Realistic outcomes per tier

- **Foundation (10-12 weeks)**: threshold shift +2-4%, VO2max +4-7%, submax HR -3 to -6 bpm.
- **Progression (8-10 weeks)**: threshold shift +3-5%, VO2max +6-9%, submax HR -5 to -8 bpm.
- **Push (8-10 weeks)**: threshold shift +4-6%, VO2max +4-8% (ceiling effect), submax HR -5 to -10 bpm.

## KPIs the app defends

- 20-min TT threshold shift (Block 2 headline)
- Submax HR at fixed pace (secondary)
- Resting HR (background trend)
- 2K row / 5K run TT (modality-specific)

## Weekly structure — evidence-scaffolded

- 4-5 sessions/week, 30-105 min each (widest range of the three in-build programs)
- Two hard sessions per week (never three, except Push tier's optional Z3)
- 48h between hard sessions non-negotiable
- Z1 volume floor rises across the block (Bishop 2019)
- Weeks 9-10 taper + retest (Mujika 2000)

## Why Block 2 is the strongest-backed of the three

The primary-source literature for aerobic training is orders of magnitude larger than for calisthenics skill programs. Every session in Block 2 has an RCT or a mechanism study behind it. The engineering choices are limited to schedule shape (weekly alternation, expansion cadence, tapering ratio) rather than to fundamental drill selection.

This matters for the founder's positioning: Block 2 can be defended in a science-advisor review with confidence. First Strict Pull-Up and Muscle-Up require more "coach consensus" flags. Sequence the ship order accordingly.
