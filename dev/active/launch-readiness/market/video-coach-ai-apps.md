# Video form-coach apps: existing players (accessed 2026-10-27)

## Facts
- Sensai 2026 roundup, no lab testing by authors: AiKYNETIX barbell lifts ~$12.99/mo, vendor "~95% accuracy" unverified; CueForm squat/bench/deadlift, metered paid tier ~$10/mo; FormCheck AI 30+ exercises ~$12-13/mo; Strive bodyweight, "depends heavily on camera placement and lighting"; Zing Coach bodyweight/machines ~$18.99/mo, no accuracy figure. https://www.sensai.fit/blog/best-ai-workout-form-check-apps-2026
- Peloton form camera is hardware-gated (Bike+, Tread+, Row+ only); no published validation found; reviewer says it catches major breakdowns but misses grip width and lat engagement (search snippet only, page returned 429). https://www.gimmie.ai/blog/peloton-iq-review-ai-coaching-features-is-it-worth-it
- Zing Coach: mixed reviews; one 5.7/10 review cites billing complaints and questions body-scan accuracy in poor light. https://wiseneuron.com/review/zing-coach (snippet, not fetched)
- Kemtai: no shutdown or acquisition found; appears to have pivoted toward clinical/healthcare (Kemtai Care, weight management, XRHealth partnership Jul 2025). https://kemtai.com/faqs/ · https://hlth.com/insights/news/xrhealth-partners-with-motion-analysis-platform-kemtai-2025-07-28
- Calisthenics: Titans Grip roundup is written by the publisher of the app it ranks first; gives no accuracy figures for muscle-up "transition" feedback. https://www.titans-grip.com/blog/best-calisthenics-app-2026/
- Validation evidence: JMIR mHealth 2025, 44 students, 12 phone setups, measures repetition detection, not form grading. Squat detection ranged 0% (side, 90 cm) to 95.5% (diagonal, 200 cm); authors list movement quality as future work. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12978916/
- Not found: independent accuracy tests for Onyx, Sency, Tempo, Exer, Hudl Technique, Metric VBT, Gymscore; no shutdown news for Onyx or Sency; no pull-up or muscle-up grading study.

## What it means for Terav
- No independent evidence that any consumer app grades pull-up or muscle-up form to a stated margin; Terav's "3 cm under the bar" claim would be unvalidated until tested on our own footage.
- Camera placement dominates accuracy, so a capture-quality gate (distance, angle, lighting) that refuses to score bad views is needed before any margin is shown.
- Competitors sell at roughly $10-19/mo, mostly for bodyweight or barbell coverage; a paid form check must justify its price against that band.
- Start with slow, camera-friendly skills; dynamic skills (kipping, muscle-up transitions) have the thinnest vendor evidence.
- Margin-style reporting is not something I found competitors doing, so differentiation is possible but unproven.
