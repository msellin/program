# Joint / niggle market: prehab apps and ad-claim line (accessed 2026-10-27)

## Facts
- Hinge Health sells employer/health-plan contracts with "engage-to-pay" billing; 3.4% of eligible members engaged in 2024; not generally available to individuals. https://www.physitrack.com/insights/hinge-health-vs-sword-health (accessed 2026-10-27, secondary summary of 2025 IPO disclosures)
- Sword Health: employer or health-plan sponsorship required (per CNBC, 17 June 2025, cited via the Physitrack page above); contract pricing not public. Same source.
- Kaia Health: no pricing or channel facts found. Reported $285M Sword acquisition (Jan 2026) is flagged by the source as unverified. Same source.
- Consumer mobility apps (secondhand, unverified): Pliability is subscription, reviewers call it steep, no USD figure found; Bend Pro listing shows $14.99/mo or $69.99/yr, but the App Store listing is by a different developer ("Patagonia Capital LLC"), so unconfirmed. https://unstar.app/blog/bend-stretchit-pliability-gowod-stretching-apps-ranked-2026 and https://apps.apple.com/lc/app/stretching-flexibility-bend-in/id6469007040 (accessed 2026-10-27)
- The Ready State: 7-day trial, no membership price found. https://help.thereadystate.com/help-center/benefits (accessed 2026-10-27)
- Meta Health & wellness policy: official page returned title only, no text. Third-party reading (conductatlas, July 2026): weight-loss/cosmetic category folded into broader health; enforcement focuses on claims (cure promises, outcome timelines, unsubstantiated "clinically proven"). https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-07-23-meta-ads-meta-advertising-policies-3936/ and https://adligator.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-ad-policy-update-2026 (accessed 2026-10-27). Meta Personal Attributes policy bars implying knowledge of a user's medical condition (secondary: outerboxdesign.com).
- Google Ads Healthcare and Medicines (Oct 2025 update, fetched directly): restricts prescription drug terms; enforcement from 29 Oct 2025. Page does not mention fitness, exercise or wellness. https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361 (accessed 2026-10-27). Personalized Advertising restricts targeting on physical health conditions (agency summary only, not fetched).
- EU MDR: intended purpose decides whether standalone software is a medical device (MoFo summary). MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 published 17 June 2025 (https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en, accessed 2026-10-27). Secondary sources say general fitness/wellness apps are outside MDR; rehab-of-a-condition wording is not addressed by any source retrieved. Full MDCG text not read.

## What it means for Terav
- Threat: Hinge/Sword/Kaia sell to employers, not individuals. Consumer mobility apps (Bend, Pliability, Ready State) own the "daily stretch" slot at roughly $15/mo and up, so a generic joint-fix pitch lands in a crowded free/trial market.
- Gap: no DTC product found that pairs a joint niggle with a progressive strength or skill goal and log-based adjustment. That pairing is Terav's shape.
- Lesson: frame the joint as a constraint on the focus goal ("train around it"), never as a condition to treat. Treatment or rehab-of-condition wording is where MDR and ad-policy exposure starts.
- Lesson: Meta reviews claims, not products. Avoid cure, timeline and "clinically proven" copy in ads regardless of category.
- Lesson: no retrieved source confirms the Meta or Google health rules for fitness apps. Get the primary policy text (Meta Advertising Standards, Google Personalized Advertising page) before spending on the ads.
