# Outreach — draft

Short, specific, and honest about what it is. Adapt per person; do not send the
same paragraph to four people.

---

**Subject:** Would you check 40 citations in a training app? ~90 minutes, credited

Hi [name],

I've built a training app called Terav. Each program targets one capability —
a first strict pull-up, an aerobic base, overhead mobility — and every claim it
makes cites a peer-reviewed paper.

Those citations have been checked, but only by me. The app currently tells users
exactly that: it says no outside coach or clinician has signed off anything in
the catalog. I'd like to stop having to say it.

Would you look at the [gymnastics] programs? It's about 90 minutes. I'd send you
a self-contained document — the programming, the entry tiers, the screening
questions, and 41 papers each with the specific claim it's attached to. Three
questions:

1. Does each paper support the claim I've hung on it?
2. Is anything prescribed here you wouldn't prescribe?
3. Is anything missing from the screening — who should this refuse to take?

**I'm not asking you to endorse it**, and I won't describe it that way. The app
would say what you checked, on what date, and that you flagged what you'd
change — including anything I disagree with and don't act on. That gets
published too.

If it's not your area, no reply needed. If you know someone better placed, I'd
take the introduction.

Margus
sellinmargus@gmail.com

---

## Notes

- Lead with the ask and the time. A specialist's first question is "how long".
- "Not an endorsement" belongs high up — it's what makes this answerable for
  someone with a licence to protect.
- Publishing disagreements is the part that makes it worth their name. Say it.
- One domain per person. Nobody is qualified for all four packets, and asking
  as if they might be signals you don't know what you're asking.
