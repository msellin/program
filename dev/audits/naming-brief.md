# Naming — shared brief for the three agents

## The product

An adaptive training app. Every session sharpens the plan against the
user's data. Programs are evidence-based (100+ cited studies), authored by
domain (aerobic, concurrent, skill, rehab). The core promise is
**personalisation on your weakness** — not "more sessions", not "harder
sessions", but "the right session for you today, against what you logged
yesterday and the studies the program is built on."

## The current name

**Terav** — Estonian for "sharp." Founder is Estonian; the app was born as
his personal training log while he rehabbed an anterior-hip issue. The name
works in Estonian and connects tightly to the brand's central verb:
"sharpen the plan every session." But the founder wants to test whether
an English name would travel better internationally, especially for a
CrossFit / strength audience.

**Terav's strengths to preserve if possible:**
- Ties to a physical, tactile verb (sharpen)
- One-syllable-ish, easy to say
- Connects to a monogram (T) that already has icon work behind it
- Suggests focus, precision, working on the weakest edge

**Terav's weaknesses:**
- Non-English readers can't pronounce it without help ("teh-RAHV" is the
  Estonian; English readers say "teh-RAV" or "TERR-av")
- Needs the "Estonian for sharp" explainer to make sense — clumsy
- Doesn't SEO cleanly against English keywords
- Doesn't parse as a verb the way "sharpen" or "hone" does

## Target audience

- CrossFitters, strength athletes, rehab-adjacent lifters
- 25-45 years old
- English-first, but international (US, UK, EU, Nordics)
- Reads sport-science newsletters, understands RPE, may or may not know LT2
- Values honesty and evidence over hype
- Suspicious of names that feel "wellness" or "startup-tech"

## Constraints for the name

- Must survive as a URL — some form of .com, .fit, or .app is a plus but
  not a hard requirement (we'll check domain availability separately)
- Must survive as a monogram — the current icon is a bronze T with a
  chisel-stroke gradient; a name whose initial letter carries similar
  weight is a plus
- Must survive as a verb in a CTA — "Sharpen the plan tonight" is easier
  than "Enrich the plan tonight." Imperative verbs are strong for this.
- Must survive on the box wall — CrossFitters shout program names.
  "I'm on Sharpen this cycle" reads OK; "I'm on Terav this cycle" reads
  Estonian.
- Must not obviously overlap with existing fitness brands — Whoop,
  Strava, Peloton, Zwift, Fitbod, Freeletics, Runna, etc.

## What NOT to submit

- Gimmicky compounds (SweatCore, AthleteHub, FitOptim)
- Anything that reads as vaguely medical (MedTrack, ClinicPath) — the app
  is deliberately not medical
- Anything that reads as vaguely AI/tech (NeuralFit, AI4Athletes)
- Tech-VC syllable smashups (Lyft-style)
- Anything requiring pronunciation help

## Deliverable

Each agent returns a markdown document with:

1. **10-15 candidate names**, each with:
   - The name
   - What it means / where it comes from
   - Why it fits (2 sentences)
   - Why it might NOT fit (1 sentence, honest)
   - How it sounds in a CTA ("Sharpen the plan tonight" / "Hone the plan tonight")
   - Rough domain / brand-availability read (if you know one is obviously
     taken by a big brand, note it — don't network-check)

2. **A shortlist of your top 3** with a paragraph on why those and not the
   others.

3. **One paragraph on what you'd rename it to if you were writing the
   cheque and had to decide today.**
