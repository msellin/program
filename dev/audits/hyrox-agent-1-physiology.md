# HYROX Agent 1 — Physiology & Biomechanics

*Research round for Terav catalog expansion. Angle: what actually makes HYROX
hard from a physiological standpoint, station-by-station gaps between elite
and amateur, and what the peer-reviewed literature says about the movement
patterns.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

Five actionable insights, cited to the strongest sources found in this
research round.

1. **HYROX is an aerobically-driven event with a strength-endurance overlay,
   not a strength event.** The one peer-reviewed physiological study to date
   (Brandt et al., 2025, *Frontiers in Physiology*) found VO2max was the single
   strongest predictor of finish time (ρ = -0.71, p = 0.01), followed by
   endurance training volume (ρ = -0.68) and body fat % (ρ = 0.67). Grip
   strength, muscle mass, body mass, and BMI did **not** predict performance.
   This directly contradicts the "hybrid athlete" marketing framing that
   emphasises maximal strength. A Terav program should be built around aerobic
   development first.

2. **Running accounts for ~50% of race time and is where elites separate
   themselves.** A 7-season longitudinal analysis (Frontiers, 2026) of the PRO
   and ELITE divisions found running represented approximately half of total
   race time in every season, and running was the metric with the *lowest*
   rank discrepancy vs overall finish position — i.e. the run column matches
   the finish column most closely. Elite PRO men hold 3:00-3:25/km across
   runs 1-7 on already-fatigued legs; open-division amateurs bleed most of
   their time on runs 5-8.

3. **Wall balls, burpee broad jumps, and sled pull produce the biggest
   station-level gaps between elite and average.** Wall balls has the widest
   spread on the entire course — over 6 minutes between top and bottom
   deciles on the men's side. Wall balls also produce the highest end-race
   physiological stress (HR 183 bpm, lactate 8.5 mmol/L, RPE 18/20 measured
   at the last station in Brandt et al.). This is where a Terav program can
   generate the largest absolute time savings.

4. **The heaviest stations (sled push, sled pull, farmers carry) are
   completed the fastest and are the least differentiating.** This is
   counterintuitive but well-documented. Brandt et al. reported sled push
   completed in 2.1 min, sled pull 2.6 min, farmers carry 2.7 min — the three
   fastest stations. Amateurs and elites are separated more by transition
   speed here than by station work speed. Absolute strength above a
   threshold has strongly diminishing returns.

5. **RoxZone (transitions) is a hidden 3-minute time bank recreational
   athletes routinely leave on the table.** HyroxDataLab's 825-athlete
   Utrecht analysis showed elites average 28s per transition; recreational
   athletes 52s — a 3:17 total gap across the race, with zero fitness
   improvement required. This is programming-addressable via race simulation
   and dry-runs, and belongs in any "race prep" module.

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 The one peer-reviewed HYROX physiology study — Brandt et al. 2025

**Citation:** Brandt T, et al. (2025). *Acute physiological responses and
performance determinants in Hyrox — a new running-focused high intensity
functional fitness trend.* Frontiers in Physiology, 16:1519240.
DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240

**Design.** 11 recreational HYROX athletes (72.7% male, median age 33,
VO2max 51 [10.8] mL/kg/min, resistance training 4 h/wk, endurance 3 h/wk).
Simulated HYROX competition with continuous HR, blood lactate at intervals,
and Borg RPE.

**Whole-race response.**

| Variable                | Overall       | Runs          | Stations       |
|-------------------------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| Completion time (min)   | 86.5 (14.5)   | 51.2 (14.1)   | 32.8 (6.1)     |
| Average HR (bpm)        | 170.9 (21.2)  | ~170          | 173.7 (19.5)   |
| Peak HR (bpm)           | 185 (26)      | 180 (25)      | 185 (26)       |
| Blood lactate (mmol/L)  | 6.3 (3.8) avg | 7.7 (4.6) pk  | 8.5 (5.4) pk   |
| RPE (Borg 6-20)         | 13.9 avg      | 13.4 (1.3)    | 14.4 (1.9)     |

**Intensity distribution: ~80% of race time was spent at 90-100% HRmax
(Zone 5) and ~20% at 70-90% HRmax (Zone 4).** This is an unusually high
proportion of time in Zone 5 for an event lasting 60-90+ minutes and is what
makes HYROX physiologically distinct from a 10K or a marathon (mostly Z2-Z3)
and from a CrossFit WOD (short duration, so Z5 tolerable in short bursts).
The scoping review by Rodríguez-Gutiérrez et al. (2025) breaks the same 80%
figure down further: 41.6% in Z4 (80-90%) and 50.5% in Z5 (90-100%).

**What predicted finish time (Spearman correlations):**
- VO2max: ρ = -0.71 (p = 0.01) — strongest predictor
- Endurance training hours per week: ρ = -0.68 (p = 0.04)
- Body fat %: ρ = 0.67 (p = 0.03)
- For runs only: VO2max ρ = -0.73 (p = 0.01)

**What did NOT predict finish time:**
- Hand grip strength: ρ = 0.09 (p = 0.79)
- Muscle mass %: ρ = -0.03 (p = 0.94)
- Resistance training hours: ρ = 0.34 (p = 0.31)
- Body mass: ρ = 0.02 (p = 0.96)
- BMI: ρ = -0.07 (p = 0.83)

**For exercise stations alone, none of the measured characteristics
significantly correlated with performance.** This is a strong statement:
the paper is effectively saying that among recreational athletes, station
work is a great equaliser and race outcome is decided on the runs. Small n
(n = 11) is a caveat — this needs replication — but the direction of every
finding is consistent with the sport's structure (50% of race time = running).

**Authors' classification:** HYROX is an "endurance-focused form of HIFT"
with "moderate to low requirements in terms of maximum strength,
coordination, and mobility" compared to other functional fitness formats.

### 2.2 Longitudinal PRO/ELITE performance — Brandt et al. 2026 (open access)

**Citation:** Longitudinal performance development in PRO and ELITE HYROX
competitions across the first seven competitive seasons. Frontiers in
Physiology, 2026. DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2026.1847569.

Analysed n = 11,842 women and 27,854 men across PRO and ELITE competitions,
seasons 1-7 (2018/19-2024/25).

**Median PRO time improvement over 7 seasons:**
- Men: ~13 min faster (~19% improvement)
- Women: ~17 min faster (~21% improvement)

**ELITE competition convergence:**
- Male median: 01:06:24 → 00:57:17
- Female median: 01:11:09 → 01:03:22
- Coefficient of variation collapsed from >10% (season 2) to <5% (season 7)
  in men; women 9.5% → 4.8%

**Interpretation.** The distribution has tightened dramatically. The
qualifying threshold to be competitive has moved by ~9-10 minutes at the
elite tier in 6 years. This means "elite" outcomes will keep drifting in
Terav's honest-outcome-range logic — need to date-stamp benchmarks.

**Where the gains came from.** Running improvements drove the bulk of
absolute time gains (~8-10 min at the median between seasons 4-7). Strength
stations (sled push, sled pull, wall balls) showed smaller absolute
improvements but greater rank reshuffling — i.e. lots of positional churn
without much time change. Running gains stick; station gains are noisy.
Programming implication: aerobic base earns permanent time; station skill
work is high-ROI but positionally volatile.

### 2.4 Individual station physiology — literature review

#### SkiErg (1000m)

- Analogous to double-poling on a Concept2 SkiErg. Peer-reviewed
  double-poling literature (Nilsson et al., 1998; Holmberg, 2015) shows
  upper-body VO2peak in cross-country skiers is 85-90% of whole-body
  VO2max. Anaerobic threshold in double poling corresponds to a blood
  lactate ~1.8 mmol/L above baseline (Nilsson et al., 1998).
- HYROX SkiErg is the **first station** so athletes come in fresh —
  physiologically it's a hard aerobic upper-body effort with a large
  latissimus, triceps, and core contribution, and it primes the arms and
  shoulders under lactate before Run 2.
- Elite men ~3:00-3:10; open men 4:29 median. Coaches converge on
  "5-10 s/500m slower than your 2K standalone pace." Common mistake:
  going out too hard in first 250m and blowing up.

#### Sled push (50m, 152kg men)

- Sled push has a **very high metabolic cost per unit time** and short-lived
  neuromuscular impairment (Cross et al., 2018, *Sports Medicine*; ETSU
  dissertation by Winwood). Muscle activation: quads and glutes dominate;
  linear decrease in hamstring recruitment as load rises.
- In HYROX, "heaviest stations are completed fastest" (Brandt et al., 2025)
  because they're 50m, not 500m. Sled push at 2.1 min median — high anaerobic
  glycolytic cost but short duration means lactate spike, then partial
  recovery on the following run.
- Turf friction adds ~30-40% functional load vs a smooth gym floor. Amateurs
  who train on rollers or wood underestimate the demand and pace incorrectly.

#### Sled pull (50m, 103kg men)

- Backwards horizontal pull, hand-over-hand rope. **This is where the
  elite-to-amateur time gap explodes** (3:53 for men). Two reasons:
  (a) grip endurance and technique — rope hand-over-hand under lactate is
  a specific skill; (b) the sled arrives after burpee broad jumps have
  fried the legs, so pull-technique breakdown compounds. No peer-reviewed
  literature on this movement pattern; closest analogue is horizontal
  rowing under load, which shares posterior-chain isometric core demand.

#### Burpee broad jump (80m)

- The single most metabolically expensive station by rep, and the second-
  widest elite-amateur gap (men 4:38). Burpee oxygen cost measured at
  ~3.5 mL O2/kg/rep in bodyweight burpee literature; at 10 burpees/min
  (a moderate HYROX pace) VO2 sits at ~35 mL/kg/min — running-equivalent
  effort with a broad jump added.
- Studies of bodyweight HIIT (Rodrigues et al., 2020) show burpees produce
  blood lactate ~11.2 mmol/L vs 6.9 mmol/L for treadmill running at
  matched HR — burpees drive glycolysis harder. Explains why HR at burpee
  station rides at 178-183 bpm in Brandt's data.
- Broad-jump component adds an eccentric-loaded landing that recreational
  athletes are not conditioned for — likely a hidden ankle/knee injury
  driver.

#### Rowing (1000m)

- Most-studied HYROX station in exercise physiology because rowing has 40+
  years of dedicated literature.
- 2000m rowing meta-analysis (Ingham et al., 2002; Steinacker, 1993): 67-88%
  aerobic, 12-33% anaerobic. VO2max explains ~72% of 2000m rowing variance.
- 1000m HYROX row: aerobic contribution drops slightly, anaerobic
  contribution rises (~30-40%). Still primarily aerobic.
- Lactate threshold on rowing ergo is a well-established predictor of race
  performance (systematic review, Vaquero-Cristóbal 2025, PMC11871166).
- HYROX row lands mid-race after 4 stations and 4 runs — legs pre-fatigued
  from sandbag lunges, sled work, and burpees means force production is
  down and stroke technique degrades. Programming implication: fatigued-
  state rowing intervals, not fresh 2K time trials, are the specific stimulus.

#### Farmers carry (200m, 2×24kg men)

- Least differentiating station on the course (both men and women bunch
  tightly at 2:09-2:17). Peer-reviewed literature on loaded carries
  (Winwood et al. 2014, *J Strength Cond Res*; McGill et al. 2012, *Strength
  Cond J*) shows carries drive HR into Z3-Z4 quickly (3-5 min) due to large
  muscle mass under isometric tension.
- Grip is the fatigue driver; peer-reviewed strongman carry research
  (McGill, 2012) shows one-handed carry produces similar physiological
  response to double-hand carry at 2× the load, i.e. grip is not linear.
- HYROX farmers carry weight is well below near-max grip for most trained
  athletes — meaning the station is a running-speed-with-load challenge,
  not a grip challenge. Elite differentiator here is turnaround technique
  (three 66m turnarounds inside the 200m).

#### Sandbag lunges (100m, 20kg men)

- No specific peer-reviewed data on the sandbag walking lunge. Closest
  analogues: walking lunge oxygen cost (moderate — ~30-40 mL/kg/min at
  bodyweight); backpacked/loaded walking with load ~25-33% BW increases
  metabolic cost by ~15-25% (Quesada et al., 2000).
- The sandbag sits across the shoulders/upper back. This forces trunk
  stiffness and quads take the eccentric brunt. Comes after farmers carry
  and before wall balls — so the arriving athlete has already loaded the
  posterior chain heavily.
- A NCT06080529 clinical trial (registered, ongoing) is measuring the
  metabolic cost of foundational sandbag training exercises including
  front lunges — results not yet published.
- Elite-vs-amateur men's gap ~3:30 — moderate. Technique matters (step
  length, hip drive) more than pure quad strength.

#### Wall balls (100 reps at 6kg to 10ft target, men)

- The final station. **Widest performance spread on the course.** Brandt
  et al. measured HR 183 bpm, lactate 8.5 mmol/L, RPE 18/20 at this
  station — highest physiological stress of the entire race.
- Wall ball = front squat + push press. Peer-reviewed data specific to
  wall balls is sparse. CrossFit's own "Karen" workout (150 wall balls
  for time) has produced case studies of rhabdomyolysis at extreme
  volumes; the 100-rep HYROX version is well below rhabdo threshold but
  still profoundly glycolytic.
- Medicine ball interval training research (Meckel et al., 2018) shows
  VO2 rises as complexity/duration increases within a set — consistent
  with wall balls being the last thing your body is willing to
  coordinate at the end of a 75+ minute race.
- **The station rewards rep-strategy discipline more than raw fitness.**
  Elite athletes sub-4 min through unbroken sets of 25-33; amateurs at
  10-plus minutes because sets fragment into 5s and 3s with long rests.
  This is a coachable and highly ROI-positive intervention.

### 2.5 Elite vs amateur — where the gap actually lives

Combining Brandt (2025), the longitudinal 2026 paper, HyroxDataLab, and the
Rox Lyfe Elite 15 profile:

**Elite 15 (2026) baseline athlete profile (men, published aggregate):**
- 5K: 15:38 | 10K: 32:38 | HM: 1:12:46 | Marathon: 2:37:02
- 2K row: 6:29 | 2K SkiErg: 6:45 | 20-min FTP: 357W
- 3RM squat 134kg / deadlift 170kg / bench 103kg / OHP 67kg
- Weekly training: ~19h; running mileage 40-45 mi/wk
- Median height 185cm, weight 82kg, age 32

**Where the elite advantage compounds:**
1. **Running pace at fatigue.** 3:00-3:25/km on tired legs vs amateur
   5:30-6:30/km. Gap: ~15-25 s/km × 8km = 2:00-3:20.
2. **Wall balls unbroken pacing.** Elite <4 min, amateur >10 min. Gap:
   6+ minutes at a single station.
3. **RoxZone.** 3:41 vs 6:58 total. Gap: 3:17.
4. **Burpee broad jump cadence.** Elite ~5:00-5:30, amateur 7-10. Gap:
   2-4 min.
5. **Sled pull rope technique.** Elite ~3:00-3:30, amateur 6-8. Gap:
   3-5 min.

Add: elite has 15-20 min headroom vs a mid-pack amateur, sourced almost
entirely from running-under-fatigue and two specific stations (wall balls,
sled pull) plus RoxZone.

### 2.6 Physiological demands vs CrossFit — the interference tension

- HIFT scoping review (Rodríguez-Gutiérrez et al., 2025, *Sports* MDPI):
  aerobic capacity, muscular strength, anaerobic power, fatigue tolerance
  are the four determinants of hybrid competitions. Elite CrossFit VO2peak:
  4.64 L/min men, 3.21 L/min women — comparable to trained rowers.
- HYROX vs CrossFit demand asymmetry: HYROX loads are 30-60% lighter but
  duration is 3-6× longer. Peak HR and lactate similar; total metabolic
  cost of HYROX ~2× a typical CrossFit WOD.
- Concurrent training interference (Wilson et al., 2012 meta-analysis):
  running as the endurance mode produces the largest interference
  effect on lower-body strength/power (SMD -0.3 to -0.4), particularly
  when sessions are same-day with <6h separation. HYROX athletes running
  40-45 mi/wk will lose some 1RM squat capacity — but the paper shows
  it does not predict their finish time anyway. **The interference
  effect is not a problem for HYROX programming.** For CrossFit athletes
  cross-training to HYROX, this is the key trade to accept.

### 2.7 Injury profile

- Villarroel López et al. (2025) — survey of 80 active HYROX athletes,
  ~43% reported a training or racing injury. Predominantly overuse:
  tendinopathies, joint pain.
- Distribution (from survey and practitioner accounts): knees (running +
  wall balls + lunges under fatigue), shoulders (SkiErg + wall ball
  overhead + sled pull), lower back (sled work + fatigued row).
- Sex difference reported: women more lower-limb, men more shoulder.
- Peer-reviewed HYROX injury data is thin — Dr Tate Cowley's team began
  systematic collection at HYROX Anaheim in Dec 2025; presentation
  expected March 2026 Coaches Summit. This is a genuine gap in the
  evidence base and Terav should flag it as such.
- For programming: knee-dominant fatigue tolerance, overhead shoulder
  capacity, and posterior chain endurance are the three prehab targets.

## 4. Terav-specific recommendations

### 4.1 The programming order-of-operations that the evidence supports

Given the Brandt et al. finding that VO2max and endurance volume are the
only significant predictors of finish time, and the longitudinal paper
showing running is where positional gains are permanent:

1. **Aerobic base is non-negotiable and comes first.** Any HYROX product
   should not "start" at race-specific work. Weeks 1-4 should look a lot
   like Terav's existing Engine Builder with a HYROX-specific run bias.
2. **Compromised running is the specificity layer.** Weeks 5-8. Run
   intervals immediately following a station effort — not station work
   for its own sake.
3. **Station skill work targets the three biggest amateur time-sinks:**
   wall ball rep strategy, sled pull technique/grip, and burpee broad
   jump cadence. Ignore optimising the fast stations (farmers carry,
   sled push) — they're not where amateurs bleed time.
4. **RoxZone rehearsal.** Explicit transition drills in the final 2-3
   weeks. Nearly free time.

### 4.2 Concrete program candidates

**Candidate A: HYROX Base (8 weeks)**
- *Positioning:* stepping stone from Engine Builder for someone who has
  aerobic base but no HYROX exposure. Also the entry point for CrossFitters
  crossing over.
- *Content mix:* 3× runs (Z2 easy, Z4 threshold, Z5 VO2max intervals) +
  2× strength-endurance circuits + 1× "one-station-plus-run" specific
  session per week.
- *Tiers:*
  - Foundation: build 4×1km at Z4, unbroken 30 wall balls, sled push
    at half-race weight
  - Progression: 5×1km at Z4, unbroken 50 wall balls, race-weight sled
  - Push: 8×1km at Z4, unbroken 100 wall balls (Karen sim), race-weight
    with faster splits
- *Retest metric:* half-HYROX (4 runs, 4 stations) time trial with all
  splits logged.

**Candidate B: HYROX Race Prep (6 weeks, taper included)**
- *Positioning:* the follow-on to HYROX Base OR a standalone for a
  returning racer.
- *Content mix:* 2× compromised-run intervals + 1× station simulation
  (2-3 back-to-back stations at race weight) + 1× race-pace long run +
  1× recovery session.
- *Tiers:* keyed off intake data — target finish time bracket
  (Foundation targets sub-2:00, Progression sub-1:30, Push sub-1:15).
- *Retest metric:* single "half-HYROX sim" at week 4 as taper checkpoint;
  full race at week 6.

**Candidate C: HYROX Skill Blocks (rolling 3-week micro-cycles)**
- *Positioning:* add-on for HYROX athletes plateaued at station-specific
  weaknesses. Not a full program; a modular product.
- *Modules:* Wall Ball 100 (rep-strategy + shoulder capacity), Sled Pull
  Fluency (grip endurance + rope technique), Burpee Broad Jump Cadence
  (glycolytic tolerance + landing mechanics).
- *Retest metric:* single-station time trial vs pre-block baseline.

### 4.3 Retest metrics honesty (Terav's tier-honest bar)

The Brandt paper's VO2max ↔ finish time correlation is strong (r² ≈ 0.50)
but n = 11. Retest metrics should:

- Report expected time range not a point estimate (e.g. "Foundation:
  sub-2:00 with 80% confidence given completed sessions")
- Anchor to the most recent longitudinal median (season 7 PRO median:
  ~57 min men, ~63 min women) with date-stamp
- Flag that grip strength and 1RM are not predictors — resist the temptation
  to include them as retest metrics just because they're easy to measure
- Add a "compromised 1K" retest (fastest 1K run after a wall ball set)
  because that IS the sport-specific fitness

### 4.4 Where HYROX fits in the Terav catalog

Argument for HYROX as a *variant tag* on Engine Builder + a dedicated
Race Prep module rather than a standalone track:

- Engine Builder already covers ~70% of HYROX Base content (aerobic
  intervals, threshold, easy Z2).
- HYROX-specific work is the compromised-running layer + station skill
  + rehearsal — this is Race Prep.
- CSM (concurrent) is a partial fit for HYROX Base, especially for the
  crossfitter-turned-hybrid athlete.
- Rowing 2K is directly reusable as a HYROX row micro-module.

**Recommended catalog structure:**
- Engine Builder → HYROX Base tag (adds run bias + intro station work)
- HYROX Race Prep (6-8 wk) as a new standalone module, gated by an

## 5. Open questions — where the evidence ran out

1. **The Brandt 2025 sample is n = 11 recreational athletes.** The primary
   physiology paper on HYROX has a very small sample size. Every strong
   claim in this report is contingent on results replicating in a larger
   study — none exists yet. Engineering choice: does Terav present the
   VO2max ↔ finish time correlation as fact or as "current best evidence,
   pending replication"?

2. **No published data on elite HYROX physiology.** All the VO2max /
   lactate data we have is from recreational athletes. What is the actual
   VO2max of a sub-1h HYROX athlete? Coaching accounts suggest 60-70
   mL/kg/min but nobody has measured it. Terav's Push tier ranges are
   therefore extrapolated, not measured.

3. **Sex differences are under-documented.** Brandt et al. was 72.7% male.
   The one clear sex difference in the aggregate race data (women's sled
   push is faster than men's; women lose relatively more time on burpee
   broad jumps) has no physiological study behind it.

4. **Injury data is preliminary.** The 43% injury rate from Villarroel
   López (2025) is from a self-report survey. No prospective incidence-
   per-1000-hours data exists yet. Wall balls, burpees, sled pull are
   suspected drivers but not confirmed.

5. **Compromised running has no dedicated study.** The term is
   practitioner-coined. There's related literature on running with
   pre-fatigued lower limbs (concurrent training) and pacing under
   fatigue (Marcora et al.'s central governor work), but nothing HYROX-
   specific. Terav's Race Prep programming will use these adjacent
   literatures as proxies.

6. **Sandbag lunge metabolic cost is being measured** in NCT06080529 —
   worth tracking that trial's readout.

7. **The physiological cost of the RoxZone itself.** The 24-second
   difference between elite and recreational transitions is treated as
   a "time bank" but there's no data on the recovery it does or does not
   permit. It might be that longer transitions are partially adaptive
   (necessary parasympathetic recovery for lower-fit athletes).

8. **Aerobic base thresholds for HYROX outcomes.** We have a VO2max
   correlation but not a threshold. What VO2max predicts sub-90? Sub-75?
   This is what Terav's honest-range engine wants and the literature
   doesn't yet provide.

9. **Longitudinal effects of HYROX training on health markers.** Zero
   published data. All rehab/health cross-talk from other HIFT
   literature (mostly CrossFit) is provisional.

10. **Is there a specific taper protocol?** Endurance-sport taper
    literature suggests 7-14 day reduced-volume taper. HYROX-specific
    taper data does not exist. Best current practice is extrapolation.
